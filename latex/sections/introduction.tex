\section{Introduction}

Brain tumors represent one of the most life-threatening pathologies, with gliomas accounting for approximately 80\% of all malignant brain tumors. Accurate segmentation of tumor sub-regions and classification of tumor grade (High-Grade Glioma (HGG) versus Low-Grade Glioma (LGG)) from multi-modal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans are critical for treatment planning, surgical guidance, and prognosis assessment. However, automated analysis faces several fundamental challenges: (1) extreme inter-patient variability in tumor size, shape, and location; (2) irregular and ambiguous tumor boundaries that even expert radiologists struggle to delineate consistently; (3) severe class imbalance where tumor pixels constitute less than 5\% of brain volume; and (4) the need to effectively integrate information from multiple MRI sequences (FLAIR, T1, T1CE, T2), each highlighting different tissue characteristics.

Traditional deep learning approaches typically address segmentation and classification as independent tasks using separate models, leading to inefficient feature learning and sub-optimal performance. While U-Net \cite{ronneberger2015unet} established the encoder-decoder paradigm as standard for medical image segmentation, pure CNN architectures face limitations in capturing long-range spatial dependencies critical for understanding tumor context and distinguishing between infiltrative regions. Recent transformer-based methods \cite{dosovitskiy2020image,chen2021transunet} demonstrate superior global context modeling but suffer from quadratic computational complexity and potential loss of fine-grained spatial details essential for precise boundary delineation in medical imaging.

Furthermore, existing loss functions used for training segmentation networks often fail to address the specific challenges of medical image analysis. Standard cross-entropy loss struggles with class imbalance, while Dice loss \cite{milletari2016vnet}, though widely used, does not directly optimize boundary precision or the intersection-over-union (IoU) metric commonly used for evaluation. This mismatch between training objectives and evaluation metrics can limit achievable performance.

In this paper, we propose AMT-UNet (Adaptive Masked Transformer U-Net), a novel hybrid CNN-transformer architecture that addresses these limitations through principled design choices motivated by the specific requirements of brain tumor analysis. Our key contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hybrid CNN-Transformer Architecture}: We design AMT-UNet with a CNN-based encoder-decoder featuring residual connections and instance normalization for stable gradient flow and batch-size independence, coupled with an adaptive masked transformer bottleneck that efficiently captures global context by focusing computation on tumor-relevant regions through learned soft masking.

\item \textbf{Adaptive Masked Transformer Bottleneck}: Unlike standard transformers with uniform attention across all spatial locations, our adaptive masking mechanism learns to weight patches based on tumor probability, reducing computational cost while improving focus on diagnostically relevant regions. The soft masking maintains differentiability while achieving the benefits of hard attention.

\item \textbf{Multi-Scale Feature Fusion}: We fuse features from all decoder levels before final prediction, combining fine-grained spatial details from shallow layers with high-level semantic information from deep layers. This addresses the semantic-spatial gap inherent in encoder-decoder architectures.

\item \textbf{ROI-Guided Multi-Task Learning}: Our framework performs simultaneous segmentation and classification with an elegant information flow: predicted tumor regions gate the classification input, focusing grade prediction on tumor-relevant areas. Gradient stopping prevents classification loss from degrading segmentation quality, enabling effective multi-task optimization.

\item \textbf{Unified Multi-Component Loss Function}: We design a combined loss integrating four complementary objectives: (1) Dice loss for stable region overlap optimization, (2) focal loss with strong focusing ($\gamma=3.0$) to handle extreme class imbalance, (3) IoU loss weighted $2.5\times$ to directly optimize the evaluation metric, and (4) boundary loss for precise edge delineation. Deep supervision at three decoder scales ensures effective gradient propagation.

\item \textbf{Architectural Design Choices}: We make deliberate design decisions based on medical imaging requirements: instance normalization for scanner-independence, LeakyReLU to prevent dying neurons in deep networks, learned downsampling via strided convolutions to preserve information, and CBAM attention \cite{woo2018cbam} on skip connections for adaptive feature refinement.
\end{itemize}

Extensive experiments on the BraTS 2020 dataset demonstrate the effectiveness of AMT-UNet. Our unified architecture achieves competitive segmentation performance across all tumor sub-regions (whole tumor, tumor core, edema) while providing accurate grade classification, all within a single 37M-parameter model with efficient inference suitable for clinical deployment.
