\section{Conclusion}

In this paper, we presented BrainTumNetV2, a hybrid CNN-transformer architecture with multi-task learning for simultaneous brain tumor segmentation and grade classification. Our method addresses key challenges in medical image analysis through several innovations: an enhanced U-Net with residual blocks, instance normalization, and multi-scale fusion; an adaptive masked transformer bottleneck for efficient global context modeling; ROI-guided multi-task learning that leverages segmentation to improve classification; and an ultimate combined loss function that integrates Dice, focal, IoU, and boundary losses with deep supervision to optimize multiple complementary objectives.

Extensive experiments on the BraTS 2020 dataset demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach. The architectural improvements enable better feature learning and gradient flow, while the carefully designed loss function addresses the specific challenges of multi-class tumor segmentation, including class imbalance, boundary precision, and direct metric optimization. The multi-task learning framework efficiently shares features between segmentation and classification tasks, with ROI-guided attention ensuring that classification focuses on tumor-relevant regions.

\subsection{Key Contributions}

Our main contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{SegUNetV2 Architecture:} We design an enhanced segmentation network featuring residual connections with instance normalization and LeakyReLU, learned downsampling via strided convolutions, CBAM attention on skip connections, and multi-scale feature fusion combining outputs from all decoder levels.

\item \textbf{Adaptive Masked Transformer:} We introduce a transformer bottleneck with soft masking that adaptively focuses computation on tumor-relevant regions while maintaining global context, achieving better efficiency than standard transformers.

\item \textbf{Ultimate Combined Loss:} We propose a comprehensive loss function that integrates four complementary components (Dice, focal with $\gamma=3.0$, IoU weighted $2.5\times$, and boundary loss) with deep supervision at three decoder levels, directly addressing the optimization challenges in medical image segmentation.

\item \textbf{ROI-Guided Multi-Task Learning:} We develop a multi-task framework with gradient-stopped ROI masking that enables effective knowledge sharing between segmentation and classification while preventing task interference.

\item \textbf{Comprehensive Empirical Study:} We provide detailed ablation studies validating each component, along with extensive analysis of loss functions, attention mechanisms, and computational efficiency.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Future Directions}

Several directions for future work emerge from this research:

\textbf{3D Extension.} The current method processes 2D slices independently, losing 3D spatial context. Extending to 3D processing could improve performance, especially for capturing volumetric tumor characteristics and inter-slice consistency. This would require addressing increased computational costs through efficient 3D attention mechanisms or hierarchical processing strategies.

\textbf{Uncertainty Quantification.} Medical image segmentation inherently involves uncertainty due to ambiguous boundaries and inter-rater variability. Incorporating uncertainty estimation through Bayesian deep learning, Monte Carlo dropout, or ensemble methods could provide confidence measures for clinical decision-making.

\textbf{Multi-Task Extensions.} Beyond segmentation and classification, other related tasks could be integrated: survival prediction, molecular subtype classification, treatment response prediction, or tumor growth modeling. A unified multi-task framework could leverage shared representations across all these tasks.

\textbf{Generalization to Other Modalities.} While we focus on brain MRI, the proposed architecture and loss functions are general and could be adapted to other medical imaging modalities (CT, PET, ultrasound) and anatomical regions (liver, lung, prostate). Cross-modality and cross-anatomy transfer learning could be explored.

\textbf{Model Compression and Efficiency.} For clinical deployment, reducing computational requirements is critical. Knowledge distillation, neural architecture search, pruning, and quantization could produce efficient variants suitable for edge devices while maintaining performance.

\textbf{Explainability and Interpretability.} Understanding what the network learns is crucial for clinical trust. Extending attention visualization, developing saliency methods specific to medical imaging, and providing natural language explanations of predictions could improve interpretability.

\textbf{Semi-Supervised and Self-Supervised Learning.} Medical image annotation is expensive and time-consuming. Leveraging large amounts of unlabeled data through semi-supervised learning (pseudo-labeling, consistency regularization) or self-supervised pretraining (contrastive learning, masked autoencoding) could improve data efficiency.

\textbf{Federated Learning for Multi-Center Studies.} Medical data is often distributed across institutions with privacy constraints. Federated learning would enable training on multi-center data without sharing raw images, potentially improving generalization and robustness.

\textbf{Active Learning for Annotation Efficiency.} Automatically identifying the most informative cases for manual annotation could reduce labeling costs while maximizing model performance. Uncertainty-based or diversity-based active learning strategies could be integrated.

\textbf{Longitudinal Analysis.} Many patients undergo multiple scans over time. Incorporating temporal information to track tumor progression, assess treatment response, or predict outcomes represents an important clinical application.

\subsection{Concluding Remarks}

BrainTumNetV2 demonstrates that careful architecture design, attention mechanisms, and loss function engineering can significantly improve medical image analysis. The hybrid CNN-transformer approach combines the strengths of both paradigms: local spatial processing with global context modeling. The multi-task learning framework efficiently shares representations between related tasks. Most importantly, the ultimate combined loss directly addresses the specific challenges of medical image segmentation through complementary optimization objectives.

We believe this work provides a strong foundation for future research in medical image analysis. The modular design allows individual components to be improved or replaced, while the comprehensive experimental methodology establishes rigorous evaluation standards. By addressing computational efficiency alongside performance, our method takes practical deployment considerations seriously.

As deep learning continues to advance medical imaging, we hope BrainTumNetV2 contributes to the development of reliable, interpretable, and clinically useful AI systems for brain tumor analysis. The code and trained models will be made publicly available to facilitate reproducibility and enable future research.
